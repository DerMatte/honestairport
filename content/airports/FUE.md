# FUE Airport Guide

> Fuerteventura’s holiday gateway is a single Aena terminal that works smoothly most days and briefly turns into a northern-European processing plant when winter-sun banks stack. Know you are at **FUE**, bring an empty bottle, and use Tiadhe guaguas or the signed taxi rank instead of improvising curb touts.

## Quick Facts
- Civil passenger airport **Fuerteventura** (also known as **El Matorral**), IATA **FUE** / ICAO **GCFV**, operated by **Aena**, about **5 km** southwest of **Puerto del Rosario** in **El Matorral**
- Aena presentation figures for **2025**: **6,886,935** passengers, **55,342** operations, **333.8** tonnes of cargo (Spanish Wikipedia, citing Aena, notes ~**+6.8%** passengers vs 2024)
- Single passenger terminal of roughly **92,000 m²** with published design capacity around **8.2 million** passengers per year; **66** check-in desks (including special-baggage positions), **24** boarding gates and **12** jet bridges; **14** arrivals belts
- Active commercial runway **01R/19L ~3,406 × 45 m** asphalt; parallel **01L/19R ~2,200 × 45 m** is published as **closed**; field elevation about **25 m / 83 ft**
- Open for scheduled commercial traffic as a **24-hour** civil airport; UK/Germany-heavy leisure network plus inter-island links (commonly **Binter** / **Canaryfly** toward **LPA** and **TFN**)
- Canary Islands are in the **Schengen** area but **outside the EU customs/VAT territory** — budget time for customs rules on goods even when passports are not the bottleneck

## Security & Screening Tips
- Standard Spanish/Aena aviation security: **100 ml** liquids rule unless a lane is explicitly posted otherwise; laptops and large electronics out when asked
- **Fast Lane** (Aena VIP): preferential security access roughly **05:45–22:30**, ticket **€11.10** adult on [serviciosvip.aena.es](https://serviciosvip.aena.es/vip/en/aeropuertos/fuerteventura); business / some elite tickets may already include it — check before paying twice
- Peak pain is less exotic scanner tech and more **volume**: winter-sun Saturdays and multi-flight UK/German banks
- Non-Schengen departures still need **passport / EES** time after the post-security lounge/retail zone — that queue can dominate the morning even when the X-ray line looks short
- Arrive about **2 hours** before Schengen/EU leisure departures off-peak; **2.5 hours** for peak non-Schengen holiday banks

## Best Airport Tricks & Hacks
- **Confirm FUE — not ACE, LPA or TFS — before you book the taxi.** Multi-island itineraries and generic “Canary Airport” web results mix codes constantly.
- **Empty bottle through security, refill at Aena fountains.** Aena explicitly lists drinking fountains under other services at Fuerteventura Airport — cheaper than airside retail when the leisure banks hit.
- **Pre-buy Fast Lane only for the ugly banks.** If FIDS already shows a pile of early northern-European departures, the **€11.10** Fast Lane QR is cheap insurance; midday midweek often does not need it.
- **Jable beats paying Aena day rates if you already have Priority Pass / LoungeKey.** Aena’s own adult day entry is **€45.95** (child **€21.25**); programme access is usually better value. Typical Aena max stay is about **3 hours** before departure (PP commonly mirrors a 3-hour cap).
- **Lounge geography:** Jable sits **airside after security** in the departures boarding zone. If you need passport control, do not cut lounge time so fine that you re-queue for the gate.
- **Tiadhe guaguas from the signed airport stops** — official Aena bus page points to Tiadhe timetables. Confirmed airport stops include **line 3** (Puerto del Rosario ↔ Caleta de Fuste / Las Salinas), **line 10** (Puerto del Rosario ↔ Morro Jable) and **line 16** (Gran Tarajal ↔ Puerto del Rosario). **Line 6** (Corralejo) is a capital-side corridor that does **not** list an airport stop — do not assume every island bus serves FUE.
- **Taxi = signed rank only.** Aena’s taxi page: always use the terminal rank, ignore drivers soliciting elsewhere, and demand a receipt. Fares are metered under Cabildo / Canary regulation with day/night and airport supplements — use the official rank or a regulated local taxi dispatch app rather than hall touts.
- **Car-hire desks melt** when three holiday flights arrive together. Have the booking open offline and photograph the car in daylight before leaving the lot.
- **Customs ≠ passport.** Flying to/from mainland Spain you may still hit green/red customs channels because the Canaries are outside the EU fiscal territory — do not wave restricted goods through on autopilot.

## Terminals & Navigation
- **One** passenger terminal on a single campus at El Matorral (the historic Los Estancos field is long closed — do not navigate by old guidebooks)
- Typical departures flow: check-in / bag drop (landside) → security (/ Fast Lane) → airside retail / Jable lounge → Schengen gates or passport control for non-Schengen → boarding (jet bridge or remote stand)
- Arrivals feed baggage reclaim (14 belts; special-baggage belts called out on airport plans), customs when applicable, official **taxi** rank, **Tiadhe** bus stops and car hire
- Walking distances are moderate for a ~7M-passenger leisure airport; the failure mode is **underestimating passport/EES queues** after security, not a multi-terminal train ride
- Follow overhead **Schengen / No Schengen** and gate signage — the commercial spine after security serves both flows

## Lounges, Food & Amenities
- **Jable VIP Lounge** is the dedicated passenger VIP lounge: airside after security in the departures boarding zone
- Priority Pass commonly lists **06:00–21:00**; Aena day entry is sold at **€45.95** adult / **€21.25** child on the official VIP shop — trust the door board for last-flight extensions
- Published Aena/PP positioning is snacks and drinks, Wi-Fi and a quieter wait — **no showers** on the published amenity lists (unlike some larger Aena leisure lounges)
- Airside F&B is holiday-airport standard: chains, coffee, grab-and-go. Eat a real meal landside or use Jable when you have access
- Free **Aena Wi-Fi** via captive portal after connecting; fine for messaging and boarding passes
- **PRM / Sin Barreras** assistance is bookable through your airline and Aena — arrange ahead for peak winter banks

## Water & Hydration
- Aena lists **drinking fountains distributed throughout the airport** under other services — empty bottle through security, then refill
- Look for fountain pictograms near toilet blocks on the departures / boarding level before paying airside retail prices
- Airside shops and vending remain the backup at roughly **€2–€3** per 500 ml

## Ground Transport & Parking
- **No passenger rail** — the island tool is **Tiadhe** (Cabildo concession / NEX Continental) guaguas plus taxi and hire car
- **Bus (Tiadhe):** Aena’s bus page defers to official Tiadhe timetables and fares ([tiadhe.com](https://tiadhe.com/todas-las-rutas/)). Airport stops appear on **line 3** toward **Caleta de Fuste**, **line 10** toward **Morro Jable**, and **line 16** toward **Gran Tarajal**, all also linking **Puerto del Rosario**. Buy on board / via Tiadhe payment products; Canary resident discounts exist for eligible travellers — tourists pay the ordinary fare. Always re-check the day’s PDF/sheet before relying on a last evening bus to the south.
- **Taxi:** Signed rank at **arrivals, floor 0**. Metered tariffs regulated locally (day/night, airport supplement). Aena warns against off-rank solicitations; keep the receipt. Rough capital rides to Puerto del Rosario are short (~5 km); south-coast resorts (Costa Calma / Morro Jable) are long metered runs — ask the rank for an estimate before you commit if budget-sensitive.
- **Car hire:** Major desks landside; queues spike when several UK/German flights land together
- **Parking:** Aena/parking.aena.es products for short and long stay — pre-book in peak winter weeks; follow terminal signs for kiss-and-fly / drop-off limits
- **Driving:** Airport sits on the FV-2 corridor south of Puerto del Rosario — easy by island standards, but allow extra time when a ship day and a leisure bank coincide in the capital
